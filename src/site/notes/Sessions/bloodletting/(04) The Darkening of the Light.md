# Summary

## **Chapter 16: Are You There?**
Monday, January 8th.

Andy and Reggie decided, after a day's rest, to tell each other about what they had been through the night before. It was as the Irishman recalled how he had used his car to pacify his frenzied childe that they heard a knock at the door: the Lord of the French Quarter himself, Savoy, coming to visit Reggie on matters concerning his business, Blood Haven. He had sent his ghoul, Julia Lansdale, a mortal well-known within the city's charity circles, to run the blood bank in the interim while Wendy was missing.

Savoy insisted on coming in, which Reggie failed to politely refuse, and asked Reggie to feed Lansdale: they'd be working together from now on, so she should be drinking his Vitae. Savoy then revealed that he knew something had happened the night before: his contacts in the NOPD had reported a "hit and run," and he seemed to know even more. Pressing Reggie for the truth, guaranteeing he was not in the business of punishing Kindred for breaking the Traditions, and promising he only wanted to help, Reggie decided to tell the Lord what had happened with Wendy. He brought Savoy to the bedroom, explaining her symptoms and how lost he had felt since her Embrace. Savoy admitted to having never heard of anything like her condition, but told him that Doc Xola in the Tremé might be able to help.

On his way out, Savoy added that if Blood Haven were to scale, there was a vampire Reggie had to meet first: one Pierpont McGinn. The Lord told him he could arrange an invitation to the manse of Pearl Chastain, the Invictus Priscus of the city. Reggie agreed to go.

## **Chapter 17: Whisper Sounds so Familiar**
Tonight, Elvys was to play his first gig in over two years. It was a small show at an even smaller dingy bar called the Blind Mule. He arrived at the cramped, musty place and spoke to the barman. When asked about his instrument, Elvys realized he had nothing to play with, but thankfully, there was a guitar waiting for him in the lost and found. A little out of tune, but he could work with that.

The show went quite well, Elvys felt, even though only a few of the bar's patrons seemed to pay attention. During his set, however, he noticed a vampire had come in and taken a seat in the back. When Elvys wrapped up, the stranger invited him to sit. This was Maldonato, the Prince's Seneschal, who had come to check on Elvys's progress in luring Shadowman out. Maldonato remarked that although the set was "charming," it would hardly do the trick. He reminded Elvys that if Shadowman was not destroyed by Saturday, it would be the musician who would face the Prince's wrath.

The reminder drove Elvys to seek out Sundown and press him for more details. He found him at Leon's, which Elvys managed to get into on the strength of New Orleans' nightlife still remembering his niche but memorable act from before his Embrace. Sundown was able to answer his questions. He revealed that Shadowman had only stopped coming to Leon's when Pearl Chastain, a famous Kindred singer, was asked to stop performing after a forty-year career during which she had not aged a day. The Prince apparently argued that to keep playing would threaten the Masquerade, but as far as Shadowman was concerned, she was the best blues singer in the city, and there was no reason to keep coming back without her.

## **Chapter 18: Like a Ghost**
Andy arrived in the French Quarter to continue his search for Caitlin Meadows, but was quickly interrupted by Maldonato, who had come to bring him in for an immediate audience with Prince Vidal. Regardless of how he felt about it, Andy knew better than to refuse.

At Perdido House, Vidal informed Andrew that there had been a brutal attack the night before. An unknown vampire had broken into the haven of the Kindred band Conqueror Worm and tried to destroy them. Fortunately, only two were left mangled, and all were still living. Andrew feigned ignorance, of course, but curiously, Vidal told him that none of the attacked vampires had recognized their assailant, though they had provided a description: a black man in his thirties. Vidal seemed to realize, even as he said it, that the description matched Andrew's. He appeared to disregard it, however, and asked Andrew to focus on finding this dangerous individual. Andrew was forced to take the job, but he was left to wonder why Joey did not tell the Prince that he was the one who tried to destroy him.

As he left, Andrew reflected on a curious impression from his meeting with Vidal: the Prince seemed to care just a little too much about Conqueror Worm and its members. He inferred there had to be far more to their relationship than Vidal let on.

## **Chapter 19: Where the Moon Changes Faces**
Early in the night, Amélie Telfort had quite the unwelcome guest at her herbarium. It was Father Marrow, Antoine Savoy's Hound, exactly the sort of man he'd send to destroy someone he considered an enemy. She feigned ignorance when Marrow questioned her, and after a few questions and a look around the herbarium, the Father apologized for the intrusion and thanked her for her time. Amélie felt briefly relieved, until she glanced at the clock. Despite the visit lasting no more than fifteen minutes, it looked like an hour had passed.

She knew instantly that the Father had done something to extract information from her without her realizing it. Worried that Caroline's location might have been compromised, she rushed to the old chapel where she had hidden the Lupine, only to realize, too late, what a terrible idea it was to come here right after Marrow's visit. She turned back and instead left a note at the spot she and Caroline had agreed to use for covert communication. It read only: _Danger._ 

Back at her herbarium, Amélie received a second set of visitors, this time, welcome ones: Baron Cimetiere, accompanied by Lidia. As arranged the night before, the three of them traveled to Bayou St. John to investigate whatever it was that the Baron had found using the maps Amélie's grandmother had drawn. At the marked point, they found a cabin. Near the cabin, they found a hole in the ground: a tomb. Amélie identified Choctaw pottery inside, which told them that the Choctaw had, long ago, entombed something here.

Then, suddenly, all three dropped to the ground. The Vitae in their bodies felt as if it were boiling off. The pain was excruciating. When it stopped, they limped out of the hole. The Baron, though he was keeping it to himself, seemed to understand something more about what they had found, and whatever it was, he looked shaken.

Outside, however, they were met with a horrifying scene: a plague of insects had formed around them, a hurricane of mosquitoes and horseflies. Then came the growling, feral dogs, barking, ready to pounce. It was clear to all three of them that this was a curse: they were being punished for going somewhere they were not meant to be.
