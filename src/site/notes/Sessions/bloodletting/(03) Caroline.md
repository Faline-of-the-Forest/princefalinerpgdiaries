# Summary

## **Chapter 11: When I Opened up my Eyes**
Hours ago, Reggie brought his childe a box full of blood bags. It's true that the bagged stuff isn't as nourishing as getting it straight from the tap, but he brought enough to last Wendy a week, if not at least a few nights.

Yet, there she was, crying his name from the bedroom. "Reggie... I'm so hungry..."

The bed, the carpet, her nightgown were still soaked in the blood Reggie had brought her an hour or two ago. What had she done with all of it? How could she still be hungry?

But the Embrace is different for all, Reggie remembered, so maybe if he headed back and grabbed a second box from Blood Haven, maybe then she'd be okay.

Reggie used his Kindred aptitudes to put Wendy to sleep, then drove back to Blood Haven to grab a second serving. He remembered that tomorrow morning, in about 8 hours, Wendy would not be able to open the blood bank, so while he was there, Reggie put up a sign indicating that the shop would be closed for the day.

As he did, he heard the shutting of a car door far behind him, and surprisingly, the voice of Antoine Savoy calling out to him. Reggie was confused to see the Lord of the French Quarter here, but Savoy was quick to admit that he had someone keeping an eye on him. In fact, he pressed, he wanted to confirm Reggie's presence at the dinner he was hosting for Desdunes, and make sure that he planned on endorsing him. In times like these, Savoy repeated, it was important they looked after one another.

Reggie had better, more pressing things to attend to at home, so he tried to quickly dismiss Savoy by agreeing, but the Lord noticed the sign on the door. The blood bank director had to lie that Wendy had suddenly left for vacation, leaving him with no day staff. Savoy looked shocked, but reminded him that he was willing to help: he believed, he repeated, that Reggie's operations were very important for New Orleans, and that it would be his pleasure to assist him. Savoy said he could find Reggie someone before morning, someone experienced and reliable. At first the director declined, but Savoy insisted. Reggie couldn't find a way to politely refuse, so he gritted his teeth and handed him the keys.

After Savoy returned to his car, Reggie did the same, hurrying to get back home.

## **Chapter 12: You Were Only Passing Through**
Amélie had instructed Caroline to stay in the basement. Down there, there were no windows, and the door locked from the inside. The herbalist knew that if a vampire truly saw Caroline change, and if that vampire was indeed looking for her as the Lupine affirmed, she wouldn't be able to keep Caroline here. But for now she had to think, and while she did, Caroline would be tucked away in the basement.

The bell at the door rang as someone walked in. It was midnight, an hour after Caroline had rushed in. Amélie could tell right away this was the vampire after her.

Amélie did not recognize him, or rather, she might have met him in the past, but she did not know him. The vampire wore a long leather coat and a wide leather hat. The man's chest, neck, and face were mangled with deep, parallel lines split into his skin. Yet, he wasn't bleeding.

The man took a moment to look around the shop, as if he were only here running errands, but he finally looked to the shopkeeper and asked if she knew anything about a Lupine nearby. Amélie lied and said that she didn't, but the man didn't buy it. He warned her: he doesn't report to Vidal, and if she cooperated now, it would stay between the two of them. However, if she refused and Vidal got involved, she would be executed.

Amélie denied it still. She didn't know what he was accusing her of, she said. The man left, warning her that she was making a horrible mistake.

When he was gone, Amélie knew Caroline wouldn't be safe here anymore. She didn't have any acquaintance who would hide a Lupine for her, she thought at first, and yet in desperation she tried to call in a favor with the only person who could help.

Calling on their blood bond, Amélie was able to make a strong connection with her sire, Rosa Bale. For a few minutes, they were able to exchange words. Amélie explained her situation, and Rosa felt at first that it was too much to ask. As much as she didn't share the Kindred's distaste for Lupines, it was a lot of trouble her childe was asking her to get into. Amélie attempted to sweeten the deal by admitting that on the next night, she was going to investigate Bayou St. John with Baron Cimetière, the man Rosa venomously detested, her only true rival, and promised to let her know everything she found out about his intentions. Rosa seemed to already have an idea of what the Baron was doing, but she agreed to the deal.

Not even an hour later, Amélie was bringing Caroline to a chapel on the edge of what was once Storyville. It was an old chapel, so imbued with True Faith that no Kindred could remain inside for more than a few minutes without going mad. This, Rosa pointed out, would be Caroline's best chance at safety. The young Lupine hugged her gracious host, teary-eyed, thanking her for everything, before retreating into the old chapel.

## **Chapter 13: Like a Sad Hallucination**
The night John Dresley died, the corpse saw its own diseased, ugly face and decided to bury itself. The musician desperately wanted to come back to his home, to his fiancée, but he couldn't do this to her; present her with what happened to her darling Elvys.

Earlier tonight, John learned that this decision had killed his dear Virginia, along with the child he hadn't known was resting in her womb. He roamed the streets, the words of his former lover echoing in his head: Virginia was dead, and Eleanor was something new, something that had never loved him. To imagine her held at the waist by this kid, Joey Tran. it infuriated him. Yet there was nothing he could do about it.

Elvys noticed a silhouette standing far away, down the street, only for a moment before it disappeared. All he saw was a shadow, but from its shape he could tell who it was.

A moment later, a hand grabbed his shoulder and flipped him around: Donovan. The Hound held him by the collar.

"The Prince lets you go, and the first thing you do is take drugs and watch a show?" Donovan clocked the leftover cocaine in John's system, which he had taken from a man in the Venue's bathroom. John had little to say in his defense.

Donovan cut straight to the chase: Vidal had given him until Saturday to find Shadowman, but if John kept making a mockery of the Prince's trust, Donovan was not going to be as patient.

After the Hound left, Elvys suddenly felt like going to see Sundown and asking him about playing at one of his clubs.

He found the Afterhours King at the Sasparilla Club, a small strip club hidden in a corner of the French Quarter. Sundown was polite, but clearly not charmed by the musician, who he found was asking a few too many questions, especially about Shadowman. Sundown told him the producer is a fan of the blues, and had been quite keen on coming to Leon's, the French Quarter's finest blues club, up until a few months ago. Elvys asked if he could, perchance, get a spot at Leon's, just for one night, he said, but it was clearly too big an ask.

Instead, Sundown told Elvys he could play tomorrow night at the Blind Mule, and said that with time, if he proved himself to be a good act, maybe he'd get to play at Sundown's better clubs. While the offer wasn't what he was looking for, it was better than nothing, and John Dresley shook on it.

After Sundown left him, Elvys decided to pay for a private lap dance as a means to feed. However, after drinking from the stripper in the private room, he was instantly caught by Sundown, who politely reminded him that feeding in the Quarter required permission he didn't have, and that he should therefore get out.

## **Chapter 14: Like a Memory in Motion**
Andy was still roaming the French Quarter looking for Caitlin Meadows, the Prince's former Hound, now apparently gone rogue. It was around midnight that he felt a knot in his chest and stomach: it was Joey, he sensed, his blood brother, in a Frenzy. It had been over a decade since he last felt something like this, but before leaving New Orleans, it would happen frequently. Joey had a knack for getting himself into intense situations, and his emotions always made it through to Andrew.

Joey Tran: his best friend, and the vampire who killed his family. The reason he came back home.

Andrew didn't hesitate to tap into the blood bond, using it to get a sense of Joey's location. He could tell Joey had slaughtered a few mortals, and he could feel his sorrow, expressed through echoes of a memory they both shared, the incident in Quang Tri, back in 1967, when Andrew and Joey's unit shot down eleven Vietnamese civilians, including four children. Andrew knew that night had forever changed Joey, and it did not surprise him that those memories haunted him every time he took mortal lives.

Andrew remembered, suddenly, being hit with that memory too, almost 20 years ago, the night his family was killed, hours before he learned about it. Joey, he thought, must have been struck by the same echo then as well.

It only took Andrew a few hours to find the warehouse Joey was using as a haven. He didn't use a moment to think

The tracker kicked the door open and pushed in. The warehouse was pitch black inside and smelled of fresh death. He first noticed a pair of eyes he didn't recognize, and the stranger roused the Beast within him. The Kindred staring at him had nothing to do with his rage, and yet she took the beating for it.

Andrew could not contain his anger as he rushed and attacked her. She was a dark-haired woman, someone he had never seen before. Moments later, two more strangers jumped at his back and tore into his body with their teeth. Andrew held his own as best he could, unable to snap out of Frenzy, and he managed to take hold of the woman and tear off one of her arms.

What finally shook him out of Frenzy was being tackled by a fourth vampire, who pushed him through the door and down onto the concrete.

"What the FUCK are you doing?!" shouted Joey.

Andrew was pinned to the ground, and suddenly he could feel the pain from the various bites taken out of his body. He gritted his teeth.

"You killed Linh. You killed Marie."

Joey didn't reply, but the look of horror on his face admitted to everything.

Knowing he would die without it, Andrew put himself back into Frenzy voluntarily, rousing the Beast and using it to push through the pain and make it out alive.

Trying to incapacitate Joey, Andrew grabbed his arm and managed to rip it off as well, all while the other vampires mauled him. The tracker did as much damage as he could, but he knew that another minute here would mean his death, so he pushed Joey off and ran as fast as he could. Surprisingly, he managed to outpace the others on foot, and, feeling the Beast laying down within him, knew he needed a good rest.

## **Chapter 15: You Were Trapped Inside**
Reggie was driving home when he glanced out his window and noticed it was wide open. Beneath it, a few steps to the side, Wendy was on all fours on the sidewalk, crawling in a pool of blood, taking hungry bites at what was clearly a fresh corpse. Reggie pulled up beside the scene and ran out. He pulled her by the shoulder, calling her name, but what looked back at him looked nothing like Wendy.

Her expression was twisted and monstrous, her eyes white and cloudy, and blood dripped from her maw onto her nightgown, which Reggie now wasn't sure had ever been white.

After he put his hand on her, Wendy tried to bite him, causing him to push her away. Something hurt in his chest: fear, primordial fear, but he couldn't quite tell why. Something about Wendy, about the thought of her biting him, agitated the Beast inside him. He tried to restrain her, but Wendy slipped away and started to sprint down the street.

Reggie didn't think: he jumped into his car and drove up to her, catching up quickly, and as Wendy tried to dodge out of the way, Reggie struck her with the car, launching her to the side. When he stopped and looked back, she wasn't moving. He grabbed her limp body and carried her inside his home, then headed back out to deal with Wendy's victim. He put the body in the back of his truck and drove to the Mississippi, where he dumped it.

When he came back, he happened to arrive at the same time as Andrew, who looked three quarters to his Final Death. Andrew didn't feel like explaining beyond the fact that he'd had a rough night. He noticed the blood on Reggie and asked about it, but Reggie, likewise, only felt like saying he'd had a rough night.
