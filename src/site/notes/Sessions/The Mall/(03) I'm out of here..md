# Summary

## Chapter 10: "Them"
The mall has heated up.

Since the gang turned off the A/C, the mall has turned into the inside of a hot car in July, and the temperature makes itself evident on everyone's skin as humidity and sweat cling to their bodies.

Maggie has turned the second floor into her camp. They convinced William to let them into the security room, which they now use to monitor the mall. They raided the food court on the third floor and created a stash of food, and looted Swords & Stuff to arm themselves.

Downstairs, hours ago, a monstrous beast composed of what looked like a cluster of dogs slaughtered a third, and potentially half, of the people trapped in the mall. This, they believe, was an attack from Casper and his friends, who many saw sneak into the Pet Emporium to unleash the beast on them.

Maggie comforts the survivors, letting them know that given the sheer number of people looking for them, the friends and families of the hundred or so people trapped inside, the police, and surely the military, are certainly working on getting them out. All they have to do is stick together and stand by.

William, the floral-shirt-wearing old man who originally called dibs on the security room, thanks Maggie once again for letting him eat from their rations. He apologizes for refusing to let them in the security room before, but admits that alone in there, he would have starved to death.

Seemingly out of nowhere, Maggie pulls out her revolver and shoots William in the back of the head. Her people scream and panic, and a man runs to her and tries to pry the gun from her hands.

"Wait!" she shouts, and everyone freezes, still listening to her even though she has just murdered a man in cold blood.

"Wait," she repeats, now at her normal volume.

She flips William's body onto his back with the tip of her shoe, and moments later, something like a long, fleshy caterpillar pushes itself out of the man's mouth. Without hesitation, she brings her shoe down on it and crushes it. Everyone looks shocked.

She addresses the group: William was one of "Them", she could tell he was different. She theorizes that something must have crawled through the vents and replaced him while he was locking himself in the security room. That's why he suddenly agreed to let them in: so he could be among them and turn them too.

People are stunned. Nobody could tell, and yet Maggie clocked him right away. She explains her background in psychology, her knack for detecting these sorts of things. That makes sense, everyone thinks, and while they are unsettled by the thought that one of "Them" was among them for the past few hours, at least with Maggie here, she'll be able to tell if there are any more still hiding.

As people disperse, Maggie looks back down at William. She noticed the hole in the back of his neck, hidden behind his collar, as he approached her: that's how she really knew, but if people are willing to believe she can simply sense these creatures out, that is an advantage she intends to keep.
## Chapter 11: Graveyard
The fire that Beth's mallrats started in the vents has died down, and the teens are getting ready to sleep. The vents are now quiet, which tells them the creatures have at least been chased off for a while thanks to the smoke.

Zeke and Newt know their friends are still stuck in the mines, but growing exhausted, they trust that Gayle and Casper can tough it out a few hours longer, and decide to rest.

In the mines, Gayle and Casper are completely, utterly lost. With only Gayle's flashlight to guide them, they follow the twisting passages, hoping to find a safe place to rest or any way out.

They come across a figure first, a person covered in dirt and mud, bashing what turns out to be the dead, dried corpse of a creature killed days ago, with a stone. The moment the mud-covered man spots them, he charges, and in self-defense Casper swings a pickaxe and kills him instantly. As the body hits the ground, it becomes clear he hadn't been turned: he was just a regular person, likely driven mad. Going through his pockets, they find his student ID. He was just another boy their age.

Pressing on, they find something strange: a bedroom door with a sheet taped to it, spelling out the name "Gayle" in a child's handwriting. Stepping inside, the mall cop finds what appears to be a perfect replica of her childhood bedroom: the piss-yellow walls, the fuzzy carpet, the flower-print bedsheets, all her childhood toys. She is exhausted, and despite the strangeness of the room, decides to take advantage of the bed and rest while Casper stands watch.

While she sleeps, Casper looks around and tries the room's second door, the one they didn't come through. He expects it to open onto a hallway, but on the other side is the cemetery right next to the Carpenter Valley Mall. Outside. It is still dark, and the gravestones, his favorite resting spot,call to him, but he feels the door must be a trick, just like the room, and closes it. He settles in to wait for Gayle to wake up.

As he waits, Casper realizes that the door they came in through has vanished. There is no longer any way out. He decides to wait anyway, to make sure Gayle gets her rest. Fighting the urge to sleep, he dozes off for just a few minutes, and has a vision, like before, of Mr. Neil in a writhing pit of flesh. This time it lasts longer. He watches Mr. Neil try to pull himself free before the flesh wraps around his bare body and drags him deeper. Mr. Neil apologizes, sobbing, and lets himself be absorbed. Casper understands: however the Mall Manager ended up here, he clearly is now in there against his will.

Gayle finally wakes up, and Casper delivers the news: their exit has vanished, and the only door left clearly leads into a trap. Gayle thinks it's worth the risk: what if it's actually a way out? Wouldn't they be crazy not to try?

They step through and make it outside. It is morning, and they are in the graveyard. In the distance they can see the edge of the Carpenter Valley Mall parking lot. They feel the morning breeze on their arms, the dew under their feet, and Casper spots his favorite spot beneath the shade of a tree.

This time, Gayle takes the watch. A few hours later, she spots a car coming up the road toward the mall. She runs to flag it down, and when it stops she sees it's Billy, the Assistant Manager. Billy looks shocked to see her outside, mentioning the hundred people that were trapped in last night, who the cops and firefighters have been trying to get out, and asks how she made it out. Gayle points to where the door let them through: it had remained there when they crossed, floating in the air with no wall behind it, then disappeared the moment they looked away. Pointing at nothing, Billy is confused, but assumes she and the mall clerk who made it out are still shaken from the ordeal.

After waking Casper, the two of them climb into Billy's car as he drives toward the mall, curious to see what it looks like from the outside. Their walkie-talkie still works, and they radio their friends with the good news, waking them both up. They are outside, they say, and they are going to get everyone help.

Newt is ecstatic. When he hears the Assistant Manager is in the car, he asks to speak to him right away, and shouts about the monsters in the mall, how many people have already died, but whenever Billy tries to respond, all Newt and Zeke hear back is static. For some reason, even though Gayle and Casper can hear every word Billy says, his voice doesn't come through on their end.

Casper has a bad feeling immediately. Without thinking it through, he opens the car door and rolls out.

## Chapter 12: Public Enemies
Confused about their friends' whereabouts and starting to doubt they are actually out of the mall, Zeke and Newt decide to stick to the plan: get inside Mr. Neil's office and find the keys to the bunker.

Getting to the second floor, however, proves to be a problem. At the stairs, two of Maggie's people block the way and shout at them to stay back: they know what they are, and they are not allowed up. Their friend, the guards say, tried to stab Maggie, and then they unleashed the beast on them, slaughtering thirty or more of the people on the first floor.

Newt insists it wasn't what it looked like, and that they have no affiliation with Casper, hence why they are without him, and asks to speak with Maggie. The men  call out for her, and moments later, the woman in Gucci glasses appears with her revolver pointed at the two of them.

She has every reason to believe they have been turned, and for that she will not let them up. In fact, she adds, if they come back here, she will shoot them both. Newt thinks to argue, but Zeke holds him back. The stairs are clearly not an option anymore.

Back on the first floor, they find another problem: the beast from the Pet Emporium is now roaming around the Orange Julius, blocking the first floor entirely.

They could always head back downstairs, but that doesn't get them to Mr. Neil's office. The vents are still smoky, so crawling through those also feels like a bad idea.

They are out of options.

Meanwhile, down in the mines, Casper wakes up inside a wall of flesh, his face and one arm sticking out, the rest of him slowly being absorbed. With no light and no room to move, he claws his way out with the sharp end of his pickaxe. He knew from the start it was a trap, but he snapped himself out of it in time. Patting the wall of flesh in the humid dark, he can tell Gayle is nowhere nearby. She is somewhere deep inside.

He takes his pickaxe and starts digging in, stopping only when he feels Gayle's limp hand. He pulls. It takes effort, but after a few tries he drags the mall cop free. Miraculously, Gayle seems unharmed. As they shake the slime and flesh off themselves, catching their breath, they hear a rumbling from a nearby passage. Gayle's flashlight beam catches it: a wave of corpses pushing through the corridor like a tsunami of flesh. Both of them run, pushing deeper into the mines as the flashlight begins to flicker, the battery nearly dead.
## Chapter 13: Exit?
Newt is fast, he tells Zeke, and he thinks he can slip past the guards and make it to Mr. Neil's office without being caught. It feels like a desperate measure, but they both feel that the others, stuck in the mines, will not make it if they can't find that key.

Newt sneaks up the stairs, and when he is close enough, he darts between the two guards and runs for the third floor. Up top, the door to Mr. Neil's office is right there. He kicks the doorknob off and rushes in, crouching below the side window so Maggie's people can't see him. Inside the manager's office, he notices a lot of locked drawers and cabinets, but what catches his attention is a strange fungus growing on the office chair.

He hears Maggie's people rushing upstairs, scattering to find him, so he decides to take his chances with the vents. He climbs onto the desk and starts unscrewing the vent cover with a card from his wallet, but someone spots him through the window, and people rush in to grab him. Newt tries to squirm free, but he is overwhelmed as a dozen people drag him out of the office, throw him to the floor, and start kicking him. He is "one of Them," they all say. A kick connects with his gut and he hears something break, and despite his best efforts, he is pinned as more people pile on to beat him to death.

Back downstairs, Zeke can tell his friend is likely in bad shape, and decides he needs to create a big distraction. He throws a handful of firecrackers toward the first floor to distract the beast, which gives him a short window to dash out of the staircase and into the Sam Goody, where he was working less than a day ago. He pulls the gate down and locks it, knowing he has maybe a minute before the beast breaks through.

He grabs as much paper as he can and starts several small fires around the store. The Sam Goody catches quickly. Zeke coughs through the smoke as the beast breaks through the door, then immediately recoils from it. Zeke drops to the ground and tries to crawl out, but the creature is waiting in the alley, ready to pounce the moment he emerges. He tries to think of another way out, but the smoke is too much. He passes out.

Back upstairs, Maggie pushes through to the scene and shouts at the pile of people to get back, revolver out. She tells them to make sure the boy doesn't go anywhere, but everyone looks uncertain.

"What is it?" she asks.

The pile disperses. There is no boy underneath. No Newt. Somehow, despite being buried under a dozen people, he slipped away. Maggie shouts at all of them: how did this happen, how did nobody see him leave?

Newt finds himself where he was, but different.

He's still on the ground, beside Mr. Neil's office on the third floor, but it's darker. The lights are off. Around him, nobody. The mall is empty, as if it were closed for the day. Through the windows, he can see the sky, no wall of flesh blocking the view. Newt gets up, shakes himself off, and walks down.

On the first floor, a dozen search and rescue workers and a few dogs are scattered. The moment they spot him, they all spring into action, rushing toward him, and moments later he has a blanket over his shoulders and can hear a dozen radios going off, announcing that a kid made it out.

Outside, the sunlight hurts his eyes. In the parking lot, about a hundred people sit in camping chairs, all facing the mall's entrance. Every head turns when he emerges, but only two of them don't look disappointed to see it's Newt. His mom and dad, eyes full of tears, run to him and hold him close. They are so happy, they say. So happy he is okay.

Newt has no idea what is happening. Did he actually make it out?

He thinks to let his friends know, and reaches for his walkie-talkie, but pulling it out, he sees it is broken. That's what he heard crack when they were kicking him.

Well, shit.
